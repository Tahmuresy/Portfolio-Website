About the project

Mondrian Generator

Draw a few rough lines. A small neural network reads what you meant, straightens it into the language of Piet Mondrian, and completes the painting around you — balancing harmony against faithfulness to your marks.

It is the software half of an ongoing project on human–robot co-authorship: the same engine is being built to guide a UR20 collaborative robot that completes, on real canvas, a painting a person has started.

How a composition is made

  1. Partition. A Mondrian process (Roy & Teh, 2008) recursively divides the canvas with horizontal and vertical cuts; larger planes are more likely to divide. Density sets its budget.
  2. Selection. Many candidates are scored on balance of plane sizes, variety of proportions, density and the absence of near-miss lines — the most harmonious is kept. Refinement sets how many.
  3. Colour. Planes are coloured largest first; touching planes never share a colour; red, blue and yellow each appear once the canvas is rich enough.

How your marks are read

  1. Recognise. Each stroke is described by 16 measures (straightness, closure, turning, corners, back-and-forth motion…) and classified as a line, path, outline, fill or dot by a 4.7 KB neural network that runs in your browser.
  2. Fit. Paths are split at their corners and every piece is snapped to horizontal or vertical. Diagonals become an orthogonal step — Mondrian rejected the diagonal.
  3. Consolidate. Re-traced lines merge, nearly-level lines align, line ends meet the nearest line or edge, colour regions snap to your lines.
  4. Complete. Your lines count as cuts the process already made, so it adds less where you have drawn. Correction trades harmony against fidelity.

96.4% of strokes read correctly on 900 held-out drawings by real people · trained on 15,000 synthetic strokes · tap Reading to see — and correct — every interpretation.

Shaped canvases

Mondrian painted lozenges, and ovals in 1913–14. The other shapes extend the same rule: the grid stays horizontal and vertical; the edge of the canvas does the cutting.
